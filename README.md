# Curatorial Judgment Recorder (CJR)

An open-source tool for documenting human curatorial decisions before cultural records enter digital archives and AI systems.

## Purpose

Every cultural archive is shaped by human decisions made before any computational system encounters the record. A curator decides what to keep, what to discard, what to show, and what to hold back. These decisions determine the form of the data that AI systems will eventually process. Yet the reasoning behind curatorial selections is rarely documented in a structured way.

The Curatorial Judgment Recorder (CJR) provides a lightweight framework for curators to document their archival decisions as they make them. The tool captures not just what was selected, but why, producing a structured layer of human judgment that accompanies the archive into any downstream system.

## Conceptual Framework

CJR is grounded in empirical research on curatorial data formation conducted across thirteen exhibitions over three years. The framework identifies distinct logics that shape how curators construct exhibition archives. Full documentation of the framework will be released upon publication of the source study.

## Intended Users

- Student curators in exhibition practice courses
- Museum educators building digital archives
- Digital archive practitioners in GLAM institutions (Galleries, Libraries, Archives, Museums)
- Researchers studying curatorial decision-making and AI-mediated cultural systems

## Current Status

In development. The conceptual framework is complete, grounded in peer-reviewed empirical research currently under review. Prototype design is underway.
